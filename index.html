What this shows

Across 1,795 Virginia schools, higher chronic absenteeism went with lower math pass rates (r = −0.412) — a moderate negative association, stronger than in Prince William County alone (r = −0.327). It is an association, not proof that one causes the other.

About these numbers

Definitions. Chronic absenteeism is the percentage of students missing 10% or more of enrolled days. Math pass rate is the percentage of students passing, Academic Achievement – Math, All Students.

How the averages are made. Division and state averages on this page are unweighted means across schools in this dataset — each school counts the same, whatever its size. The VDOE-published enrollment-weighted statewide chronic absenteeism rate for 2023–24 is 16.1%, which differs from the state average above because it weights schools by enrollment.

One school excluded. Key Center (Fairfax County Public Schools) is not in this dataset: its math pass rate is suppressed as “<” in the source file, so it has no math rate to pair with its absenteeism rate. The dataset covers 1,795 of the 1,796 schools in the source files.

Reading the chart. These are school-level figures and the relationship is correlational: it shows an association between absenteeism and math results, not that absenteeism causes lower scores.

Source: Virginia Department of Education, School Quality Profiles, 2023–2024 (Chronic Absenteeism and Accreditation Rates files, via Data export.zip).
